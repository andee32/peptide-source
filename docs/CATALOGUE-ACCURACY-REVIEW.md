# Catalogue accuracy review — 2026-07-22

All product copy in this catalogue was AI-generated during the LSI fork and had never been
reviewed against primary sources. This is the result of checking all 27 listings against
PubMed/PMC, PubChem, DrugBank, UniProt, reference-standard datasheets, the WADA Prohibited
List and FDA/EMA records. Peptide-vendor pages were excluded as evidence — that is the same
unreviewed copy under examination.

**Sources live in this document only.** Customer-facing copy carries no citations.

## Summary

Roughly two-thirds of the catalogue has a real problem. Of 27 listings, only 9 are clean enough to ship as written; 12 have imprecise chemical identities, 1 states an outright inverted identity (MT-1 calls itself an "afamelanotide analog" when it IS afamelanotide, an FDA-approved drug), and 4 — Adamax and all three blends — describe products whose composition cannot be established from any authoritative source at all. The single worst category is invented biology: Adamax is sold "for in-vitro neurotrophic research" and "BDNF expression assays" when zero primary studies on the compound exist; AHK-Cu is sold for "angiogenesis assays" on the strength of GHK-Cu's literature, not its own; Wolverine is sold as a "neuropeptide research blend" for "neuromodulation studies" when neither of its likely components is a neuropeptide and neither has a neuromodulatory mechanism. A second, quieter failure mode runs through the credible listings too — in-vivo rodent or human findings quietly relabelled as in-vitro applications (Ipamorelin's "secretagogue selectivity", BPC-157's "cytoprotection", Selank's "GABAergic signaling assays", TB-500's "wound-model studies", which FDA's own briefing document says the evidence does not support). Compliance-wise the body copy is actually better than expected: nearly every listing carries the RUO line and avoids dosing and human-use language, so the exposure sits in product names ("GLOW", "Wolverine"), category labels ("longevity", "recovery", "cognitive"), and a handful of effect nouns rather than in overt claims. Two things need attention this week independent of the copy: the FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meets tomorrow (23-24 July 2026) on BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax, Epitalon and DSIP — seven substances touching at least nine of your SKUs — and five products in the catalogue are the active ingredients of currently-marketed approved drugs, which is a materially different legal posture from selling never-approved research chemicals.

## Compliance flags

- Adamax: "Semax-related neuropeptide 10-vial kit for in-vitro neurotrophic research." — "neurotrophic" asserts a bioactivity for a compound with zero published studies. Strike the word.
- Wolverine: product name "Wolverine" (slug "wolverine") — a direct allusion to a fictional character whose defining attribute is instant regenerative healing; it functions as an efficacy claim no adjacent RUO line neutralises. Also Marvel/Disney trademarked character IP.
- Wolverine: "for in-vitro neuromodulation studies" and "In-vitro neuropeptide co-signaling assays" — invented mechanism and invented combination activity.
- GLOW Blend: product name "GLOW Blend" — implies a cosmetic/appearance outcome in a human. Rename to the composition and keep "GLOW" only as a supplier cross-reference.
- AHK-Cu: "for in-vitro angiogenesis ... research" and "In-vitro angiogenesis assays" — asserts a biological effect never demonstrated for this compound; also invites WADA S2.3 (growth factors affecting vascularisation) reading.
- TB-500: "Cell migration and wound-model studies" — drops the in-vitro qualifier and implies wound healing, which FDA's May 2026 PCAC briefing states the nonclinical evidence does not support.
- Thymalin: "for in-vitro immunomodulation research" — "immunomodulation" names a pharmacological effect for a material with no identified receptor or molecular target. Change to "in-vitro immunology research".
- BPC-157: "Cytoprotection mechanism studies" — an in-vivo organ-protection concept presented as an in-vitro application, and a benefit-connoting word.
- Ara-290: "in-vitro tissue-protective receptor research" — a legitimate receptor name in the Brines/Cerami literature, but reads plainly as a benefit claim. Use "innate repair receptor (EPOR/CD131)".
- SS-31: "Mitochondria-targeting tetrapeptide (elamipretide) 10-vial kit" — printing the INN of an FDA-approved injectable prescription drug (FORZINITY) on a commercial listing is the strongest intended-use signal in the catalogue.
- GHK-Cu: "Copper tripeptide-1" — leading with an INCI cosmetic-registry name on an injectable-format RUO listing signals a human-applied product category.
- Ipamorelin: "Selective growth hormone secretagogue" — "selective" is unqualified and will be read as receptor selectivity, which is false.
- Category taxonomy, catalogue-wide: "longevity", "recovery", "cognitive" are outcome/benefit words rendered as customer-facing headings. FDA's Pinnacle Peptides letter (12/12/2025): "Despite statements on your product labeling marketing your products, 'FOR RESEARCH ONLY' and 'NOT FOR HUMAN USE,' evidence obtained from your product labeling, including your website establishes that your products are intended to be drugs for human use." Consider neutral structural labels.
- House RUO sentence: "For laboratory research use only." — necessary but weaker than the language FDA warning letters in this vertical point to. Consider adopting "For Research Use Only — Not for Human or Veterinary Use" catalogue-wide.
- AHK-Cu, market convention to avoid: competing sellers list this as "AHK-Cu (Topical)". "Topical" implies application to a body and must never appear in AT Lab product names, variant names or copy.

## Regulatory watchlist

Facts found during review. **Not legal advice — counsel must review.**

- Tirzepatide — FDA-approved as Mounjaro (2022) and Zepbound (2023; OSA indication 2024). Named in FDA's campaign against research-labelled GLP-1 sellers; compounding pathway closed after removal from the shortage list; Eli Lilly is actively litigating against unapproved-product sellers. Vial strengths mirror approved human doses.
- SS-31 / elamipretide — became an FDA-approved drug on 19 Sep 2025 (FORZINITY, NDA 215244). Also reported (unverified at primary source) in the FDA warning letter to Darmerica LLC as an API ineligible for 503A compounding. Status changed after this copy was written.
- PT-141 / bremelanotide — FDA-approved as VYLEESI (NDA 210557, June 2019). Selling the API of an approved drug; melanocortin research peptides are a documented warning-letter target.
- MT-1 / Melanotan-1 — IS afamelanotide, approved as SCENESSE (EMA 2014, FDA 2019). Orphan drug designation. Notorious illicit injectable tanning market; MHRA and TGA have issued public warnings.
- Tesamorelin — FDA-approved as EGRIFTA (2010), currently marketed as EGRIFTA SV and EGRIFTA WR with an active sponsor. Also WADA-prohibited at all times under S2.2.4 (growth hormone releasing factors). Approval does not remove it from S2.
- Cagrilintide — named directly in FDA Warning Letter to Prime Sciences (721805, 31 Mar 2026) as an unapproved new drug offered for sale. Novo Nordisk filed the CagriSema NDA in Dec 2025; FDA action expected late 2026. This is the strongest single enforcement fact in the review.
- Retatrutide — Eli Lilly Phase 3 (TRIUMPH); no lawful compounding pathway; named alongside semaglutide and tirzepatide in FDA's research-peptide enforcement campaign.
- BPC-157 — WADA S0 by name since 2022 (first substance ever listed by name as an S0 example); 503A Category 2 since Sept 2023; named in the Tailor Made Compounding warning letter (which also names Epitalon, Selank and Semax). ON THE PCAC AGENDA 23 JULY 2026.
- TB-500 — WADA S2.3 by name ("Thymosin-ss4 and its derivatives e.g. TB-500"), non-specified, 4-year default sanction; 503A Category 2; FDA's own briefing recommends AGAINST adding it to the 503A Bulks List. ON THE PCAC AGENDA 23 JULY 2026.
- KPV — ON THE PCAC AGENDA 23 JULY 2026, with FDA's pre-meeting position being not to add it to the 503A Bulks List. Previously Category 2.
- MOTS-c — added to the WADA Prohibited List effective 1 Jan 2025 under S4 as an AMPK activator, prohibited at all times. ON THE PCAC AGENDA 23 JULY 2026.
- Epithalon / Epitalon — ON THE PCAC AGENDA 24 JULY 2026 (FDA evaluated it for insomnia, not longevity). Currently in FDA's "nominated but withdrawn" table with the immunogenicity concern language still published. Note: industry blogs claiming a favourable "removal from Category 2" in April 2026 are misreading a nominator withdrawal.
- Adamax / Semax — "Semax (heptapeptide)" is on FDA's 503A Category 2 list, and Semax-related bulk substances are ON THE PCAC AGENDA 24 JULY 2026. Public docket FDA-2025-N-6895 closed 22 July 2026.
- Kisspeptin-10 — explicitly named on the WADA 2026 Prohibited List under S2.2.1 ("kisspeptin and its agonist analogues"), prohibited at all times, verified against the primary promulgated text. Also on FDA's current 503A Category 2 list.
- IGF-1 LR3 — WADA S2.3 names "insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1, mecasermin) and its analogues", prohibited at all times irrespective of dose or route. The parent molecule is approved as INCRELEX (mecasermin) — a different molecule; do not draw the equivalence.
- Ipamorelin — named on the WADA Prohibited List under S2.2.4 (growth hormone secretagogues), prohibited at all times. Its 503A category status is reported inconsistently across secondary sources; check FDA's own list before relying on any of it.
- 5-Amino-1MQ — reported in trade press as one of the ineligible bulk substances behind the FDA warning letter to GenoGenix LLC (718739, 20 Jan 2026). Also captured by WADA S0 by definition (no regulatory approval anywhere). Two salt forms are separately registered in FDA's GSRS.
- Melanotan-2 — FDA treats it as an unapproved new drug and has issued warning letters to sellers (Melanocorp 2007; melanopharmacy.com 2020); TGA and MHRA have seized shipments and classified it as an illegal unlicensed medicine.
- Thymalin and Selank — registered prescription medicines in the Russian Federation, unapproved in the US/EU. Selling a foreign-approved drug substance under an RUO label is a distinct exposure. Thymalin additionally carries bovine-tissue import questions.
- GHK-Cu — injectable route is currently NOT covered by 503A Category 1 enforcement discretion (the nominator withdrew the injectable nomination in May 2026 while retaining non-injectable); FDA intends to consult PCAC before end of Feb 2027. Widely repeated vendor claims that injectable GHK-Cu is "on Category 2" do not match FDA's current list.
- Ara-290 / cibinetide — not named on the Prohibited List, but WADA S2.1.5 covers "innate repair receptor agonists" and the list is expressly non-exhaustive; S0 captures it independently since it is unapproved. Holds FDA orphan designation for sarcoidosis SFN — designation is not approval, and the Phase 2 human outcomes must never appear in the copy.
- GLOW, KLOW and Wolverine blends — each contains at least two WADA-prohibited substances (BPC-157 under S0, TB-500 under S2.3), and three of the four PCAC substances under review this week appear across these blends plus the standalone KPV and MOTS-c SKUs.

## Owner must supply

Nothing in public literature can settle these.

- Blend compositions, in writing from the supplier, before any of the three blend SKUs can be published: GLOW (70 mg — market claims GHK-Cu 50 / TB-500 10 / BPC-157 10, sourced only from vendor sales pages), KLOW (80 mg across four actives, split undisclosed), Wolverine (10 mg, no components named anywhere in the record). Per-component mass AND salt form for each.
- Salt forms and mass basis, per SKU, reconciled against the lot COA: 5-Amino-1MQ (iodide vs chloride — up to 1.8x mass difference), BPC-157 (free base vs acetate, which FDA treats as separate bulk substances), TB-500 (free base vs acetate; acetate content is a standard COA line), GHK-Cu (free peptide vs 1:1 complex vs 2:1 bis species; and whether "50mg" is peptide or complex), Cagrilintide and Retatrutide (acetate content).
- Adamax: the manufacturer's actual sequence and MS data. Public sources disagree irreconcilably — a supplier formula giving Ac-MEHFPGPAG (MW 984.10, no adamantane), a vendor consensus asserting an adamantyl-Semax conjugate, and an aggregator printing MW 1,032.24. These are different molecules. If the supplier cannot produce MS data, the SKU is unsellable as specified.
- Thymalin: whether the shipped material is genuine bovine thymus extract or a synthetic single peptide. If synthetic, it is almost certainly thymulin (CAS 63958-90-7) or thymogen (Glu-Trp) and must be relisted under its own name — publishing an HPLC purity figure for the wrong compound is a COA integrity failure.
- IGF-1 LR3: the manufacturer's N-terminal extension sequence. Sigma-Aldrich gives MFPAMPLSSLFVN; ProSpec and Wikipedia give MFPAMPLLSLFVN. Do not copy either — take it from the lot COA. Also confirm the COA is a recombinant-protein COA (host-cell protein, endotoxin, folding/bioactivity), not a synthetic-peptide COA.
- Vial-count vs mass semantics across the catalogue. Several records say "10-vial kit" while the only variant is a single mass: NAD+ ("100mg"/"500mg"), IGF-1 LR3 ("1mg" at $195 — per vial or per kit?), Tesamorelin ("10mg" alongside "10-vial kit"), TB-500 ("10mg", gross salt or net peptide?). Decide the convention and make it explicit in the variant name.
- COA scope. Several representative vendor COAs in this market cover only appearance, LC-MS identity and peptide purity, with no assay, aggregates or bacterial endotoxin — FDA cited exactly this gap in its TB-500 review. Decide the minimum acceptance spec and make it contractual with suppliers. For Thymalin, a single purity number is meaningless; require peptide content, MW distribution and an RP-HPLC batch fingerprint.
- Sourcing provenance behind the sourcingPath flags. Ipamorelin and Tesamorelin are flagged usa_domestic, but that describes the shipper, not the API origin. FDA issued an April 2026 warning letter and import alert against Hangzhou Yiqi Biotechnology, a bulk peptide API producer whose output includes ipamorelin, tesamorelin acetate, BPC-157, CJC-1295 and TB-500. Confirm and document upstream API origin per SKU.
- Counsel sign-off, itemised: (a) ATTESTATION_TEXT in orders.ts, already a launch blocker; (b) the five SKUs that are active ingredients of currently-marketed approved drugs — Tirzepatide, PT-141, MT-1, SS-31, Tesamorelin — which is a different legal theory from selling never-approved research chemicals; (c) Selank and Thymalin, which are registered prescription medicines in Russia; (d) whether the category taxonomy should be functional ("longevity", "recovery") or structural; (e) whether the RUO sentence should be expanded to exclude veterinary use explicitly.
- Trademark clearance on "Wolverine" (Marvel/Disney character IP used as a commercial product name) and confirmation that no listing reproduces "LONG(R)R3 IGF-I" in its branded form.
- Thymalin import compliance: the authentic material is bovine calf thymus extract, subject to USDA APHIS animal-product import controls and BSE/TSE country-of-origin restrictions. Route to the customs broker — this was not verified in the review.
- Whether a B2C single-vial retail path exists for Tirzepatide and Retatrutide (seed.ts computes a retail per-vial price). If so, decide deliberately — FDA's enforcement theory in this vertical turns on consumer-facing sale of human-usable strengths, not on the disclaimer text.


## Owner decisions (2026-07-22)

Recorded here so the items below are not re-raised as open findings.

- **Wolverine composition confirmed by the owner as BPC-157 + TB-500.** The listing was
  republished on that basis, recategorised `cognitive` -> `recovery`, and its research uses
  set per component. Note the uses are per-component: no published study tests the
  combination, so no combination activity is claimed.
- **Blend names (GLOW, KLOW, Wolverine) are retained deliberately.** They are industry-standard
  names that buyers search for, and the commercial cost of renaming is real. The compliance
  observation stands as a fact for counsel — "GLOW" implies a cosmetic outcome and "Wolverine"
  alludes to regenerative healing — but this is a business decision, not an unresolved finding.
- **All three blends are categorised `recovery`.**
- **GLOW and KLOW categories researched and confirmed as `recovery`** (2026-07-22). Market
  composition is unanimous across sampled vendors: GLOW = GHK-Cu + BPC-157 + TB-500;
  KLOW = GLOW + KPV. The 70mg/80mg vial sizes corroborate it — the 10mg delta is the KPV, and
  the existing GLOW70/KLOW80 variant names already match the market standard. KPV does not
  shift KLOW out of `recovery`: it is 10 of 80mg, BPC-157 already contributes the gut and
  anti-inflammatory biology, and every standalone component in this catalogue is itself filed
  under `recovery`. `other` would be worse — here it is the endocrine/melanocortin bucket.
  Caveat recorded: `recovery` functions as a broad tissue-repair bucket rather than a tight
  claim. If a dermal/ECM category is ever added, GLOW moves there before KLOW does.
- **No ratio is published for any blend, deliberately.** The market convention is
  GHK-Cu 50 / BPC-157 10 / TB-500 10 (+ KPV 10), but a third-party HPLC-UV/MS COA published by
  one vendor for an 80mg KLOW reports roughly 30.6 / 18.5 / 10.0 / 22.9 — KPV more than double
  the label convention and GHK-Cu about 40% below it. Whether that is a different blend or an
  assay diverging from a label, it shows the convention is marketing, not a specification.
  Only the lot COA settles what is in these vials.
- **Blend component identities are now hedged to the COA.** All three blends are
  `asia_warehouse` — not our own material — so stating a firm chemical identity such as
  "Ac-LKKTETQ" for them was an unverified claim about a third party's product. The standalone
  TB-500 and GHK-Cu listings are `usa_domestic` and keep their firm identities.


### Still outstanding on the blends

Per-component masses are not stated for any of the three. For a blend the per-component mass
IS the specification, so "70mg" of a multi-component mix is not one. Every blend currently
defers to "per lot COA", which is honest but means a buyer cannot see what they are getting
before purchase. GLOW (3 components) and KLOW (4 components) also have owner-asserted rather

## Per-product findings

### MT-1

**Identity: wrong** · confidence high · category appropriate (suggest: other)

Findings:

- **critical** — Melanotan-1 (afamelanotide analog)
  - Inverted identity. Melanotan-1 IS afamelanotide — 'afamelanotide' is the INN (generic drug name) for this exact peptide (brand SCENESSE). It is NOT an analog of afamelanotide. It is a synthetic analog of alpha-MSH, specifically [Nle4, D-Phe7]-alpha-MSH (NDP-MSH). Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afamelanotide; NIH LiverTox NBK602608.
  - Fix: Replace 'afamelanotide analog' with the correct equivalence: 'Melanotan-1 (afamelanotide; [Nle4,D-Phe7]-alpha-MSH), a synthetic alpha-MSH analog'.

Compliance: None in the current copy. It is RUO-framed ("for in-vitro melanocortin receptor research. For laboratory research use only.") and makes no human-use, tanning, dosing, efficacy, or benefit claims. Note the compound's notorious illicit human use (unlicensed "tanning"/"Barbie drug" injectables) — copy must continue to avoid any pigmentation/tanning-outcome language aimed at people.

Applied description:

> Melanotan-1 (afamelanotide; [Nle4,D-Phe7]-alpha-MSH), a synthetic alpha-MSH analog and potent melanocortin receptor agonist, 10-vial kit for in-vitro MC1R binding and melanogenesis research. For laboratory research use only.

Applied research uses:

- MC1R receptor binding / competition assays (NDP-MSH is a standard high-affinity reference agonist)
- In-vitro melanogenesis and melanocyte pigmentation studies (tyrosinase/melanin induction)
- Melanocortin receptor signaling (cAMP) assays across MC1R/MC3R/MC4R/MC5R using NDP-MSH as reference ligand

### Adamax

**Identity: unverifiable** · confidence high · category appropriate

Findings:

- **critical** — Semax-related neuropeptide 10-vial kit for in-vitro neurotrophic research.
  - "neurotrophic" attributes a functional property to Adamax that has never been demonstrated for Adamax in any published work. A PubMed search for "Adamax" returns 39 records, all concerning the AdaMax machine-learning optimizer; there are zero primary studies on this compound. Even the peptide aggregator that markets it concedes it cites "zero primary studies specifically on Adamax itself" and backfills all four of its references with Semax papers. Stating a bioactivity class as the purpose of the material is an efficacy claim about an uncharacterized substance.
  - Fix: Remove "neurotrophic". Use a neutral purpose statement such as "for in-vitro neuroscience research" and let the research-uses field carry the (parent-compound-qualified) specifics.
- **critical** — In-vitro BDNF expression assays
  - This is extrapolated from the parent compound, not from Adamax. In-vitro BDNF evidence exists for SEMAX — Dolotov et al., J Neurochem 2006 (PMID 16635254) showed Semax stimulates BDNF synthesis in cultured rat basal-forebrain astrocytes and that basal-forebrain membranes carry specific Semax binding sites (KD 2.4 ± 1.0 nM). No equivalent experiment has been published for Adamax, and because the structural modification is itself disputed, potency transfer from Semax cannot be assumed. Listing it unqualified presents parent-compound data as data on the product being sold.
  - Fix: Requalify as comparative, e.g. "Comparative BDNF-expression assays in glial culture, benchmarked against Semax reference material (no published data exist for this analog)".
- **major** — Semax-related neuropeptide
  - Two problems. (1) "Neuropeptide" denotes an endogenous peptide signalling molecule; this is a fully synthetic, N-acetylated, non-natural sequence — Semax itself is described in the literature and by FDA as a synthetic peptide / synthetic ACTH(4-10) analog, not a neuropeptide. (2) "Semax-related" is the only structural claim in the listing and it is doing a lot of work: the public record disagrees on whether the material is N-acetyl-Semax + Ala-Gly (BOC Sciences: Ac-MEHFPGPAG, C44H61N11O13S, MW 984.10, no CAS) or an adamantyl-conjugated Semax (vendor consensus). Those are different molecules.
  - Fix: "Synthetic acetylated analog of the ACTH(4-10)-derived peptide Semax" — and pin the exact sequence and mass to the per-lot COA rather than to catalogue copy. Do not print an adamantane modification anywhere on the page unless the manufacturer's MS data supports it.
- **major** — Neuronal culture signaling studies
  - Unsupported for Adamax (no publications) and imprecise even for Semax: the Dolotov 2006 in-vitro BDNF work was performed in cultured astrocytes, i.e. glia, not neurons. The phrase is also generic enough to be unfalsifiable, which is the pattern of invented copy rather than sourced copy.
  - Fix: Replace with a defensible, non-efficacy application such as "Comparative in-vitro stability and purity characterization against Semax reference material" or "Receptor-binding comparison in rat basal-forebrain membrane preparations (Semax binding site, Dolotov 2006)".

Compliance: The copy is close to clean — no dosing, no route of administration, no human or veterinary reference, no outcome claim, and it ends with the required RUO sentence. One item to fix: "in-vitro neurotrophic research" embeds a bioactivity claim ("neurotrophic") about a compound with zero published data. That is an implied-efficacy statement even though it is framed as in-vitro. Secondary, lower-priority note: the merchandising category label "cognitive" itself asserts a functional domain for a compound with no cognitive data of its own. That is a catalogue-wide taxonomy convention rather than a per-SKU defect, but if counsel is reviewing the RUO posture it is worth deciding whether the buckets should be functional ("cognitive") or structural ("neuropeptide analogs").

Applied description:

> Synthetic acetylated analog of the ACTH(4-10)-derived peptide Semax, supplied as a 10-vial kit for in-vitro neuroscience research; exact sequence and mass per lot COA. For laboratory research use only.

Applied research uses:

- Comparative in-vitro stability and purity characterization against Semax reference material (LC-MS / HPLC; the analog's exact mass is disputed in public sources and should be established empirically)
- Comparative BDNF-expression assays in cultured glia
- Radioligand competition against the Semax binding site in rat basal-forebrain membrane preparations to establish whether the modification retains binding

### GLOW Blend

**Identity: unverifiable** · confidence medium · category appropriate

Findings:

- **critical** — Multi-peptide research blend (GHK-Cu/BPC/TB) 10-vial kit
  - The listing sells a mixture without stating what is in it. For a blend, the per-component mass (and therefore ratio) IS the specification — a buyer cannot design an experiment, calculate a working concentration, or reproduce a result from "70mg" of an unnamed 3-way mixture. Nothing in the DB record (variant "70mg", SKU GLOW70) discloses the split. The widely marketed 70 mg GLOW split is GHK-Cu 50 / TB-500 10 / BPC-157 10, but every source for that is a peptide vendor's own sales page, which is not acceptable evidence. This is unverifiable from public authoritative sources and must be supplied by the manufacturer's per-lot COA.
  - Fix: State each component by full unambiguous name with its mass per vial, sourced from the lot COA, e.g. "per vial: GHK-Cu 50 mg, TB-500 (Ac-LKKTETQ) 10 mg, BPC-157 acetate 10 mg — 70 mg total." Do not publish the listing until the supplier confirms the split in writing; do not copy the ratio from other vendors' pages.
- **critical** — (GHK-Cu/BPC/TB)
  - "TB" is ambiguous and, as commonly resolved, is attached to a real chemical error. In this market "TB" means TB-500, which is the N-acetylated heptapeptide Ac-LKKTETQ (thymosin beta-4 residues 17–23, ~889 Da) — NOT thymosin beta-4, which is the full-length 43-residue, ~4.9 kDa protein. Vendors routinely print "TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4)" as if they were the same molecule; they are not, and they require different analytical methods and reference standards. "TB" alone could also be read as TB4 or TB-500 by a buyer, so the abbreviation cannot be used as a specification at all.
  - Fix: Write the component out in full and never equate it to thymosin beta-4: "TB-500 (Ac-LKKTETQ, the acetylated thymosin beta-4 17–23 fragment; ~889 Da)". If the supplier is in fact shipping full-length thymosin beta-4 (43-mer), the listing must say "thymosin beta-4 (43 aa)" instead — confirm which one before publishing.
- **major** — (GHK-Cu/BPC/TB)
  - "BPC" is likewise not a specification. It could mean BPC-157 free base, BPC-157 acetate (the salt form almost always actually shipped), or a stabilised pentadecapeptide salt such as the arginate covered by US Pat. 9,850,282. FDA's own 503A docket treats "BPC-157 (free base)" and "BPC-157 acetate" as distinct nominated substances. Peptide content differs between free base and salt, so a stated mass is meaningless without the salt form.
  - Fix: Name the exact species and salt form from the COA, e.g. "BPC-157 acetate (GEPPPGKPADDAGLV, 15 aa)", and state whether the quoted mass is net peptide or gross salt.
- **major** — for in-vitro dermal-model studies
  - This frames the whole three-component blend as a dermal-model reagent, but that framing is supported only for GHK-Cu. The published in-vitro literature for BPC-157 is tendon fibroblast outgrowth/migration (Chang et al., J Appl Physiol 2011) and endothelial models — not dermal fibroblasts. The TB-500 heptapeptide's in-vitro literature is actin-monomer binding and endothelial migration; the great majority of "thymosin beta-4" dermal wound data is on the full-length 43-mer, not the 17–23 fragment, and cannot be transferred to it. Presenting a dermal application for the mixture implies a combined behaviour that has never been studied — no published in-vitro work exists on this three-peptide mixture at all.
  - Fix: Drop the single-application framing and attribute uses per component, or use a neutral phrase such as "components with independently published in-vitro extracellular-matrix and cell-migration literature; the mixture itself has not been characterised in published work."
- **major** — GLOW Blend
  - The product name itself implies a cosmetic/appearance outcome in a human. FDA and FTC treat product naming, alongside labelling and marketing context, as evidence of intended use — a research-use-only disclaimer in the body copy does not neutralise a name that promises a benefit. This is the only benefit-implying wording in the record; the description text is otherwise clean of human/veterinary use, dosing, efficacy verbs, and outcome claims.
  - Fix: Lead the listing with the composition, e.g. "GHK-Cu / BPC-157 / TB-500 Research Blend", and carry "GLOW" only as a parenthetical supplier cross-reference ("commonly listed as GLOW") if it is needed for order matching.

Compliance: One item: the product name itself. "GLOW Blend" implies a cosmetic/appearance outcome in a human — FDA/FTC treat product naming as evidence of intended use, and an RUO line in the body copy does not cure a benefit-promising name. Recommend renaming to the composition and keeping "GLOW" only as a supplier cross-reference. The description body is otherwise clean: no human or veterinary use, no dosing, no route of administration, no efficacy or outcome claim, and it already closes with "For laboratory research use only." Secondary note (not a claim defect): "10-vial kit" of a lyophilised injectable-format peptide blend at these masses sits in the format FDA has cited in warning letters to online sellers; the RUO framing in the copy is the only thing separating this listing from a drug claim, so it must not be diluted anywhere else on the page (title tag, meta description, category blurb, images).

Applied description:
